On-Page SEO: What to Fix on a Page Before You Chase Links

By Prabal Raj Shakya, Digital Marketing Specialist Updated November 24, 2026 9 min read
seoon-page seo

Key Takeaways

  • Start with intent. A page ranks when it gives the searcher what they came for. Keywords come second.
  • Seven elements do most of the work: title tag, URL, headings, opening answer, body content, internal links and images.
  • On-page is the cheapest SEO win. You control it fully, so fix it before spending on links.
  • Titles earn clicks; content earns the ranking. A good title on a thin page still fails.
  • Measure by query, not by page. Check which searches a page appears for, then edit toward the ones that matter.
From my own work: At GSA Contract Services I optimise meta titles and descriptions in RankMath, because accurate on-page metadata affects both rankings and the click-through rate from the results page. The steps below follow how I approach on-page work there.

On-page SEO is everything you control directly on a page that helps search engines and readers understand what it is about and whether it deserves to rank: the title, headings, content, internal links, images and the way the page answers the query it targets. It is the part of SEO you can fix this week without anyone else’s permission.

This guide shows what to change, in what order, how on-page SEO differs from off-page and technical SEO, and how to tell whether your edits worked.

On-page vs off-page vs technical SEO: how they differ in practice

On-page vs off-page

On-page tells a search engine what a page is about. Off-page tells it that other people agree the page is worth reading, mainly through links from other sites. A page with great content and no links can struggle in competitive searches. A page with many links and weak content tends to slip when a better answer appears. You need both, but on-page is where to begin because it costs only time.

On-page vs technical

Technical SEO decides whether a page can be found and processed at all. On-page SEO decides how well it competes once it is. If a page is blocked or not indexed, polishing its headings does nothing. If it is indexed and fast but vague, technical fixes will not make it rank.

On-page for people vs for search engines

The two goals point the same way more often than not. A clear title, a direct answer, scannable headings and helpful images serve readers and give search engines cleaner signals. The mistakes come when the page is written for the keyword: repeated phrases, padded intros, and headings that read like search queries glued together.

Where on-page SEO fits in your SEO plan

Think of SEO as a sequence. First make sure the site can be crawled and indexed (technical). Then make each important page the best answer to its query (on-page). Then earn the trust that lifts those pages above competitors (off-page).

If your technical SEO basics are broken, fix those first. Once pages are indexed, on-page changes are usually the fastest way to move rankings, and they support everything that follows, including off-page SEO.

How to do on-page SEO, step by step

Work through one important page at a time, in this order.

  1. Define the one query and intent. Search it yourself. Note what the top results are: guides, product pages, lists, videos. Match that format.
  2. Write the title tag for the click. Put the main phrase near the front, make it specific, and keep it short enough to display. One page, one promise.
  3. Write the meta description as a pitch. It is not a direct ranking factor, but a clear one improves the chance someone clicks.
  4. Use one H1 and logical H2s and H3s. Headings should tell the story of the page when read on their own. Use natural wording that covers the subtopics people expect.
  5. Answer the query early. Give the answer in the first paragraph, then add depth, examples and evidence. First-hand experience is what generic pages lack.
  6. Clean up the URL. Short, readable, lowercase, with words rather than IDs. Avoid changing a URL that already ranks unless you add a redirect.
  7. Add internal links with descriptive anchors. Link from related pages into this one, and from this page to related pages. "Our guide to local SEO" tells more than "click here".
  8. Optimise images. Compress, use modern formats, give each a descriptive file name and honest alt text, and set width and height so the page does not jump.
  9. Add structured data where it matches the page, such as Article, Product or FAQ, but only for content that is visible.
  10. Show who wrote it and when it was updated. This supports trust, especially on topics where accuracy matters.

On-page checklist you can print

  • One clear target query and intent for the page.
  • Title tag: specific, front-loaded, not truncated, unique.
  • Meta description: written for people, unique.
  • One H1; H2s and H3s that cover the subtopics.
  • Answer within the first screen of text.
  • Original examples, data or experience, not a rewrite of the top results.
  • Internal links in and out with descriptive anchors.
  • Images compressed, described and sized.
  • Mobile layout readable with no sideways scrolling.
  • Author, date and sources visible.

How to measure whether on-page changes worked

Compare like with like, and give Google time to re-crawl.

  1. Record a baseline for each edited page: clicks, impressions, average position and click-through rate for its main queries.
  2. Annotate the date you changed it.
  3. Check after four to eight weeks. Smaller changes can show sooner, but a fair test needs time.
  4. Look at queries, not only the page. A page may gain new queries even if its main one is flat.
  5. Watch engagement: scroll depth, time on page and conversions tell you if the right people are landing.

Seven on-page mistakes to avoid

  1. Stuffing the keyword. If it reads oddly aloud, it is too much.
  2. One title for many pages. Each page needs its own promise.
  3. Ignoring intent. A sales page will not rank for a "how to" query.
  4. Thin pages. Short pages with nothing original give search engines no reason to choose you.
  5. Orphan pages. Pages with no internal links are hard to find and easy to ignore.
  6. Missing alt text or oversized images. They hurt accessibility and speed.
  7. Changing everything at once. You will not know what worked.

Frequently asked questions

What is on-page SEO in simple words?

It is improving the things on a page itself, such as the title, headings, content, links and images, so search engines and readers can tell what it is about and that it answers the question well.

Is the meta description a ranking factor?

It is not a direct ranking factor, but a good one can raise the number of people who click your result, which helps in practice.

How many times should I use the keyword?

There is no ideal number. Use the main phrase where it reads naturally, cover related terms, and focus on answering the query completely.

How long does on-page SEO take to work?

You may see changes within a few weeks once the page is re-crawled. Competitive queries often take longer.

Is on-page SEO enough on its own?

Not always. It is necessary, but competitive searches also need trust signals from outside your site, covered by off-page SEO, and a technically sound site.

Conclusion

On-page SEO is the foundation you fully control. Choose a page, define the intent, write a title that earns the click, answer the question early and build depth with real examples. Then connect it to the rest of your site with clear internal links.
