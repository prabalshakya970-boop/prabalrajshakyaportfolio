How SEO Helped GSA Contract Services Grow Organic Search Clicks 8x

By Prabal Raj Shakya, Digital Marketing Specialist Updated November 25, 2026 7 min read

Results at a glance

  • Search clicks grew about 8x in the same 92-day window, from 155 in 2025 to 1,241 in 2026.
  • Average position improved from 19.5 to 10.1, about 9 places higher.
  • Active website users per day rose about 3.5x, from 11.7 to 41.4.
  • Form submissions became qualified. The earlier count of about 10 a month was mostly bots. After the relaunch the site gets 15 to 20 qualified leads a month.
  • AI Overview mentions went from 2 to 25+, more than 12x.

How did SEO help GSA Contract Services grow organic traffic? I fixed the technical errors first, gave every service its own page, and rewrote the content around direct answers. Over the same 92-day window, search clicks rose from 155 to 1,241 and average position moved from 19.5 to 10.1. The changes produced measurable results within two months of the relaunch.

Client overview

GSA Contract Services, LLC is a consulting firm that helps businesses obtain and manage GSA Schedule contracts with the U.S. federal government. The firm has operated since 1995, supports more than 250 active clients, and works with a team of Authorized Negotiators.

For most prospects, the website is the first place they learn what the service includes. The site had to explain that service clearly, answer the questions a new buyer asks, and make it easy to request a conversation.

The challenge

When the project began, the website had fallen behind both the business and the way buyers search. The main problems were:

  • The content was outdated and did not reflect the services the firm offers today.
  • Keywords were not organized. Nearly every target term pointed at the homepage.
  • No landing pages existed for individual services, so a prospect searching for one specific service had no dedicated page to find.
  • Images were low quality and heavy in file size, which slowed pages down.

Phase 1: Niche research

Before changing anything, I studied the niche. The typical reader is a small business owner, sales director, or operations manager who is trying to judge whether GSA Schedule work is worth the time and cost. I mapped the questions these readers ask, the terms they type into search, and the points where they tend to leave a site.

That research shaped two decisions. It set the keyword plan, and it determined which service pages the site needed.

Phase 2: Technical audit

The technical audit found a set of errors that were limiting the site's visibility. Each issue was documented, ranked by impact, and fixed before the content work began.

Phase 3: Keyword organization and service landing pages

I grouped keywords by service and assigned each group to its own page. The homepage kept the broad brand and category terms. Service pages took the specific terms a prospect would use when they need help with one part of the GSA process.

Each page received a single H1, a clear title tag, and a meta description written for the reader who finds it in search results. The result is a site where every important page has a clear reason to exist.

Phase 4: On-page optimization and blog revamp

I improved on-page elements across the site, including titles, headings, internal structure, and image markup. The blog received the same treatment.

I rewrote weaker posts, linked related articles to each other, and placed calls to action where a reader would naturally want the next step. Internal links helped search engines understand how the topics relate. They also gave readers a clear path from an article to a service page and then to a contact request.

Phase 5: Content framework and AI Overview visibility

The revised content followed one consistent framework. Each page opens with a direct answer, supports it with specific steps or facts, and closes by connecting the topic to the firm's services.

After the content was rebuilt on this framework, the website was mentioned in Google's AI Overviews. I believe the combination of clearer answers, stronger page structure, and tighter internal linking contributed to that result, though I cannot isolate one change as the cause.

Phase 6: Branding and site health

I applied a consistent color system across the website, so headings, buttons, and calls to action read as one product. Consistent branding makes the site feel current. That matters for a firm that asks clients to trust it with a government contracting process.

Before launch, I checked that every page, form, and link worked. A site that looks professional but breaks under use sends the wrong message to a prospect.

Key takeaways

  • Audit first. Technical errors can hide the value of good content.
  • Give each service its own page. A single homepage cannot carry every service term.
  • Keep images light and descriptive. Page speed and accessibility improve together.
  • Build each page around a direct answer, clear structure, and specific detail.
  • Link related content so readers and search engines can follow the path to the services.
  • Click-through rate is the next lever. Several high-impression queries, including TAA compliant and GSA contract, still earn clicks at well under 1%.

Next steps

GSA Contract Services has helped businesses pursue GSA Schedule contracts since 1995. A website review for any firm follows the same sequence: audit the site, map keywords to pages, rebuild the weak content, and measure the results.
